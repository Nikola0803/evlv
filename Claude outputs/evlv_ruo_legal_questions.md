# EVLV — Questions for a Regulatory/Telehealth Attorney

Prepared to make an initial consultation efficient. Organized by topic so you can prioritize based on billable time. None of this is legal advice — it's a list of what to ask, based on general public information about how the RUO exemption and telehealth space work.

## 1. Core RUO exemption — is the current setup actually sufficient?

- Does our current labeling ("For Research Use Only. Not for human or animal use. Not for diagnostic or therapeutic use.") on every product page, plus a checkout-time attestation checkbox, meet the standard the FDA/FTC would actually apply, or is that standard higher than what a website can achieve regardless of wording?
- Is there a meaningful legal difference between a **passive disclaimer** ("by placing this order you agree...") and an **active opt-in checkbox** the buyer must check? Does the active version actually reduce liability, or is it mostly theater?
- Does requiring the buyer to type/confirm an institutional or business affiliation (vs. allowing pure consumer checkout) materially change our risk profile? Is that something we should require?
- Are there specific phrases or claims (even ones that feel neutral to us) that are known enforcement triggers regardless of surrounding disclaimers?

## 2. Format-specific risk (this is the one I most need a real answer on)

- We currently sell (or are considering selling) compounds in: lyophilized powder vials (reconstitution required), pre-loaded injector pens, nasal sprays, and oral tablets.
- Is it accurate that **format itself** (not just labeling/marketing) is weighed as evidence of "intended use," and that consumer-style delivery formats (pens, nasal sprays, tablets) are much harder to defend as "laboratory research" than a sealed vial of powder?
- If that's true: is there **any** RUO-compliant way to sell pen/nasal-spray/tablet formats, or is that fundamentally incompatible with the RUO framework no matter how it's marketed?
- Would selling those formats only to verified business/institutional accounts (not individual consumers) change the analysis?
- Should we simply not carry those formats under the current business model, and hold them until/unless we have licensed prescribing infrastructure?

## 3. State-by-state variance

- Are there states where selling any of our current catalog (peptides, GLP-class compounds) to a resident is restricted or banned outright, regardless of RUO labeling?
- Do we need geofencing/shipping restrictions to certain states, and if so, which ones, and how do we find out as our catalog changes over time?
- Is there a practical way to monitor this ourselves going forward, or does it require ongoing counsel engagement?

## 4. Compounded GLP-class products specifically

- Given the FDA's shortage-based compounding exemptions for semaglutide/tirzepatide-class drugs have been lapsing through 2024–2025, what is the current legal status of selling **any** compound in that mechanism class as a "research chemical," even under a non-trademarked internal name?
- We currently market these three compounds under our own naming (not the innovator drug names). Does that meaningfully reduce trademark/FDA risk, or is the underlying mechanism-class identification itself still the exposure regardless of what we call it?
- Is there patent/trademark exposure specifically (separate from FDA/FTC exposure) from selling compounds chemically similar to patented drugs, even under a different name and RUO framing?

## 5. Marketing and content review

- We've removed outcome-based/DTC framing (e.g., "shop by goal," personal benefit language) from the site. Is there a practical checklist or standard you'd want us following for all future marketing copy, social content, and affiliate/influencer content, so we're not relying on ad hoc judgment calls?
- Are there specific FTC guidelines (beyond FDA's RUO guidance) around testimonials, before/after content, or influencer marketing that apply here even if the product labeling itself is compliant?

## 6. Business structure questions

- Does selling through an LLC/corporate structure provide meaningful personal liability protection here, or does FDA/FTC enforcement typically reach through that in this category?
- Do we need product liability insurance specific to research chemical sales, and is that even available/affordable for this category?
- What records should we be retaining (attestations, CoAs, order data, verification records) and for how long, in case of an inquiry?

## 7. The telehealth pivot (separate track, don't conflate with the above)

- If we later want to operate a licensed telehealth arm (physician-reviewed intake, real prescriptions, licensed pharmacy fulfillment) — what is the actual minimum viable regulatory path? Rough order of magnitude on cost and timeline (not exact, just so expectations are grounded)?
- Does operating both an RUO storefront and a licensed telehealth business under the same brand create cross-contamination risk (i.e., does the existence of the telehealth arm undermine the RUO defense for the storefront, or vice versa)?
- Do we need entirely separate legal entities for the RUO business vs. a future telehealth business?

---

**Context for whoever takes this call:** current business is a direct-to-consumer e-commerce storefront selling peptide compounds labeled Research Use Only, no prescriber/clinical component today. Considering (not yet building) a future licensed telehealth arm. Site currently has: RUO labeling on every product page, an RUO policy page, a checkout attestation checkbox, CoA/batch data per product, and no outcome/DTC marketing language. Format lineup includes vials (current), and pens/nasal sprays/tablets (under consideration, not yet live).
